# Character Philosophy

## Core Principle

**The Problem:** In *Blindsight*, every character speaks in impenetrable jargon with no distinct voice. They all sound like Watts' PhD thesis.

**The Solution:** Give each character a unique, recognizable voice while preserving the scientific concepts they're discussing.

**The Method:** Deep character analysis → Voice profiles → Method acting snapshots → Consistent translation

---

## Character Voice Profiles

### **Siri Keeton (Protagonist / POV)**

**Role:** Narrator, "Synthesist" - interprets between specialized crew members

**Original Problem:**
- Technical, detached, neurologically modified
- Speaks like a textbook even in moments of fear
- No emotional language despite learning to "feel"

**Translation Goal:**
- Introspective, uncertain, relatable POV
- Entry point for readers into hard SF concepts
- Human vulnerability despite modifications

**Voice Traits:**
- First person introspection
- Questions more than declares
- Accessible metaphors for complex concepts
- Emotional awareness (he's learning to feel)
- "I wondered..." instead of "The topology suggested..."

**Example Transformation:**

**Original (Watts):**
> "The phenomenological barriers prevented epistemic closure regarding the entity's intentional states."

**Translated (Sarasti Method):**
> "I couldn't tell what it was thinking. Every time I thought I understood, it did something that made no sense."

### **Jukka Sarasti (Vampire Commander)**

**Role:** Mission commander, translator between humans and aliens

**Original Problem:**
- Impenetrable jargon makes him unreadable
- Supposed to be terrifying but just sounds academic

**Translation Goal:**
- Unsettling, alien, but comprehensible
- **ONLY character who stays highly technical** (intentional)
- Predatory intelligence, not professorial

**Voice Traits:**
- Terse, commanding, predatory
- Uses jargon strategically (intimidation/superiority)
- Short declarative sentences
- Others translate/react to emphasize his otherness
- Technical language = alien language (deliberate barrier)

**Why He Stays Technical:**
- Sarasti IS the alien translator in the story
- His incomprehensibility is thematic (vampire = Chinese Room)
- Other characters' reactions show his otherness
- Readers experience him as crew does: unsettling, superior, opaque

**Example Transformation:**

**Original (Watts):**
> "Metacognitive recursion indicates suboptimal heuristic deployment."

**Translated (Sarasti Method):**
> "Metacognitive recursion indicates suboptimal heuristic deployment."  
> *(Keeton's reaction):* "I had no idea what that meant. I don't think he cared."

**Note:** Sarasti's dialogue stays technical, but POV character translates the *effect*, not the words.

### **Isaac Szpindel (Biologist)**

**Role:** Xenobiologist, most "human" crew member

**Original Problem:**
- Jargon-heavy scientist speak
- No personality beyond expertise

**Translation Goal:**
- Enthusiastic, uses metaphors, teaching style
- Natural translator of complex ideas
- Coping mechanism: explain when scared

**Voice Traits:**
- "Think of it like..." explanations
- Excited about discoveries
- Translates technical to accessible naturally
- Nervous humor under stress
- Most emotionally expressive character

**Example Transformation:**

**Original (Watts):**
> "The organism exhibits non-Euclidean topological configurations inconsistent with terrestrial phylogenetic constraints."

**Translated (Sarasti Method):**
> "Think of it like... okay, you know how all Earth life has the same basic blueprint? DNA, cells, that whole deal? This thing doesn't. It's like trying to fit a tesseract into a triangle. The geometry doesn't work."

### **Amanda Bates (Multiple Personalities)**

**Role:** Mission commander (nominally), "Gang of Four" collective intelligence

**Original Problem:**
- Technically distinct personalities but all sound like Watts
- Switching personalities not jarring enough
- Lost opportunity for dramatic voice differentiation

**Translation Goal:**
- Genuinely different personalities with distinct voices
- Personality switches mid-conversation = memorable, unsettling
- Each sub-personality has different emotional tone

**Voice Traits:**
- Distinct vocabulary per personality
- Tone shifts: diplomatic → aggressive → analytical → withdrawn
- Mid-sentence switches disrupt conversation rhythm
- Other characters react to the jarring transitions

**Sub-Personalities (Examples):**

1. **"The Peacemaker"** (Diplomatic)
   - Seeks consensus, uses "we" language
   - Smooth, professional, de-escalating

2. **"The Tactician"** (Aggressive)
   - Direct, confrontational, mission-focused
   - Short commands, no preamble

3. **"The Analyst"** (Detached)
   - Third-person observations
   - Clinical, observational

4. **"The Empath"** (Withdrawn)
   - Personal pronouns shift ("I" vs "she")
   - Quieter, hesitant

**Example Transformation:**

**Original (Watts):**
> "We should proceed cautiously. Analysis suggests risk mitigation protocols."

**Translated (Sarasti Method):**
> "We should take this slow, make sure everyone's—" Her voice sharpened mid-word. "Analysis complete. Execute now." A pause. "She's right, you know. We can't wait."

### **Robert Cunningham (Soldier)**

**Role:** Military specialist, pragmatic survivor

**Original Problem:**
- Same jargon as everyone else

**Example Transformation:**

## The Sarasti Translation Method

### Voice Consistency Rules

1. **Keeton (POV):** Translates concepts for readers naturally
2. **Sarasti:** Stays technical; others react to show impact
3. **Szpindel:** Teaching metaphors; explains while discovering
4. **Bates:** Voice switches mid-conversation; jarring but clear
5. **Cunningham:** Tactical brevity; cuts jargon to action

### Method Acting: Character Snapshots

For each chapter, define:
- **Mental State:** Where is this character emotionally?
- **Physical State:** Fed, rested, injured, stressed?
- **Current Concerns:** What's on their mind right now?
- **Voice Adjustments:** How does this chapter's context affect speech?
- **Recent Events:** What just happened that colors their reactions?

**Example (Sarasti, Chapter 3):**
- **Mental State:** Hyperfocused, predatory calm
- **Physical State:** Fed recently, optimal performance
- **Current Concerns:** Mission parameters, crew is a tool
- **Voice Adjustments:** More technical jargon (asserting dominance)
- **Recent Events:** Made eye contact during feeding discussion (crew intimidated)

This creates evolving, context-aware characters rather than static voice templates.

## Philosophy: Respecting the Author

### **Acknowledging Watts' Intent**

Watts' technical prose was **purposeful:**
- Creates alienation (mirrors theme of incomprehensible intelligence)
- Reflects Keeton's neurological modifications
- Challenges readers (not hand-holding)

**We're not saying "Watts is bad."**  
**We're saying "Watts is inaccessible to many readers who would love his ideas."**

### **Framing as Translation, Not Correction**

This is an **alternative translation**, like:
- Modern English Shakespeare
- Accessible academic papers
- "No Fear" study guides

The original remains primary. This is a bridge.

### **Preserving Core Themes**

**Non-Negotiable:**
- Chinese Room hypothesis (intelligence without consciousness)
- Consciousness as evolutionary liability
- Alien intelligence beyond human comprehension
- Hard SF rigor (no pseudoscience)

**What Changes:**
- How concepts are expressed
- Character voice differentiation
- Jargon → metaphor (where appropriate)
- Emotional accessibility (especially Keeton)
