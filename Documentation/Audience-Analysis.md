# Audience Analysis

## Primary Audience: The Bounced Readers

### Who They Are

**Demographics:**
- Age: 25-50 (adult SF readers)
- Education: College+, intellectually curious
- Reading habits: Love hard SF concepts but limited time/patience
- SF experience: Read Clarke, Asimov, but bounced off modern technical SF

**Why They Bounced Off Original:**
- "I got lost in the jargon by page 20"
- "Every character sounded the same"
- "I needed a neuroscience dictionary just to follow dialogue"
- "Loved the concept, couldn't finish the execution"
- "Felt like reading an academic paper, not a novel"

**What They Want:**
- Big ideas about consciousness, AI, alien intelligence
- Characters they can distinguish and care about
- Prose that flows (don't have to re-read sentences)
- Atmosphere and tension (space horror should be gripping)
- Feel smart reading, not confused

**Success Metric:** 
> "I finished the Sarasti Translation in a week and immediately wanted to try the original again. Now I understand what Watts was going for, even if the prose still challenges me."

## Secondary Audience: Educators

### Who They Are

**Contexts:**
- College courses: Philosophy of Mind, AI Ethics, SF Literature
- High school: Advanced SF/Philosophy electives
- Book clubs: Adult discussion groups
- Online courses: AI safety, consciousness studies

**Why Original is Difficult:**
- Students can't get through it (DNF rate high)
- Discussion stalls on "what did this sentence mean?"
- Brilliant for thesis topics, terrible for classroom engagement
- Want to teach Chinese Room concept, original makes it inaccessible

**What They Want:**
- Readable version to assign as primary text
- Original as supplementary "challenge reading"
- Discussion questions that focus on ideas, not parsing prose
- Side-by-side comparisons for teaching literary analysis
- Bridge to Watts' original for motivated students

**Success Metric:**
> "70% of students finished the Sarasti Translation. 40% went on to read the original. We had the best discussions about consciousness vs. intelligence this class has ever had."

## Tertiary Audience: Existing Watts Fans

### Who They Are

**Characteristics:**
- Already finished and loved *Blindsight*
- Appreciate the difficulty (badge of honor)
- Evangelize the book but acknowledge it's hard
- Want more people to experience Watts' ideas

**Why They Might Embrace This:**
- "I want my friends to read this, but I can't recommend it without caveats"
- "A translation means more people get Watts' ideas"
- "Cool experiment—does AI 'get' what Watts was doing?"
- "If this brings Watts new readers, I'm for it"

**Why They Might Reject This:**
- "You're dumbing down a masterpiece"
- "The difficulty is part of the art"
- "Watts wrote it this way intentionally"
- "Fanfic is disrespectful"

**How to Win Them Over:**
- Frame as "supplement" not "replacement"
- Publish system prompt (transparent method)
- Invite their feedback during development
- Position as "gateway drug" to original
- Acknowledge Watts' intentional craft choices
- Lead with respect and humility

**Success Metric:**
> "I still prefer the original, but this translation got three of my friends into Watts who wouldn't have finished otherwise. That's a win."

## AI Ethics Community

### Who They Are

**Contexts:**
- AI safety researchers
- LLM developers and researchers
- Philosophy of mind academics
- Tech ethics bloggers/podcasters

**Why This Project Matters to Them:**
- *Blindsight* predicted LLM behavior in 2006
- Chinese Room hypothesis is central to AI consciousness debates
- Practical demonstration of LLM capabilities and limitations
- Meta-commentary: Using Chinese Room to translate Chinese Room fiction

**What They Want:**
- Case study in AI-assisted creative work
- Transparent methodology (reproducible experiment)
- Discussion of what LLMs "understand" vs. pattern-match
- Educational resource for teaching Chinese Room concept
- Example of responsible AI use (tool for access, not replacement)

**Success Metric:**
> "The Sarasti Translation is now required reading for my AI Ethics seminar. It's a perfect case study in LLM capabilities, limitations, and the ironic philosophical implications."

## Non-Goals

### Who This is NOT For

**Literary Purists:**
- Believe any alteration is sacrilege
- "Death of the author is death of art"
- Will never accept derivative works

**Anti-AI Activists:**
- Oppose all AI creative applications
- See this as job theft from human translators
- "AI can't create, only steal"

**Watts Completionists Who Love the Prose:**
- Think the technical writing is brilliant
- Enjoy parsing difficult sentences
- "The challenge is the point"

**We're Not Trying to Convert These Groups:**
- Acknowledge their perspective
- Original remains available and primary
- This is additive, not subtractive
- Different strokes for different folks

## Edition Naming Strategy

### **2025 Edition** (Recommended)

**Format:** *Blindsight: The Sarasti Translation — 2025 Edition*

**Rationale:**
- Accessible (no technical version numbers on cover)
- Allows future updates without v2.0/v3.0 confusion
- Year-based = professional publishing standard
- Leaves room for "2027 Edition (Revised)" if needed

**Internal Versioning:**
- Colophon can note "v0.5 Beta" for techies
- Repository tags: v0.5-beta, v1.0-release
- Don't expose version complexity to general readers

---

### Alternative: No Version Marker

**Format:** *Blindsight: The Sarasti Translation*

**Rationale:**
- Clean, simple, timeless
- No implication this is "unfinished" (beta sounds incomplete)
- Avoid version number proliferation

**If Project Continues:**
- Add year only if significant revision: "2027 Edition"
- Or model-based: "GPT-5 Translation" (if different LLM used)
